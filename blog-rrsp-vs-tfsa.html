RRSP vs TFSA: Which Should You Contribute to in 2025?

May 2025 · 5 min read · Accountant Kitchener

Every year, Kitchener residents face the same question: should I contribute to my RRSP, my TFSA, or split between the two? The answer depends on three key variables: your current income, your expected retirement income, and your short-term financial needs.

How Each Account Works

An RRSP (Registered Retirement Savings Plan) lets you deduct contributions from your taxable income today, reducing the tax you owe in the current year. The money grows tax-free inside the plan, but you pay income tax on withdrawals in retirement.

A TFSA (Tax-Free Savings Account) offers no upfront deduction, but all growth and withdrawals are completely tax-free, forever. Unused contribution room carries forward indefinitely.

The Core Rule: Compare Your Tax Rates

The RRSP wins when your tax rate today is higher than your expected tax rate in retirement. The TFSA wins when your tax rate today is lower, equal to, or uncertain relative to your retirement rate.

When the RRSP Wins

  • You are in a high income tax bracket (above $100,000)
  • You expect lower income in retirement
  • You want to reduce taxable income to qualify for income-tested benefits
  • You are using the Home Buyers' Plan or Lifelong Learning Plan

When the TFSA Wins

  • You are in a lower or middle income tax bracket
  • You expect significant retirement income from other sources
  • You may need to access the money before retirement
  • You receive income-tested government benefits such as GIS or OAS clawback

Can You Contribute to Both?

Absolutely, and many Kitchener clients do exactly that. A common approach is to contribute to the RRSP up to the point where the deduction reduces your income to the next tax bracket, then direct remaining savings to the TFSA.
